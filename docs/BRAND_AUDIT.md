# Brand Audit

## Current identity

- `AI Fleet OS` is explicitly described in README as the current product codename, not a final approved name.
- The UI lockup uses `AI FLEET OS` and the descriptor `Decision & execution layer`.
- Current product language spans command center, fleet management, project production, routing, local-first execution, and evidence-based delivery.

## Current audiences

Evidence in product flows supports these audiences:

- developers managing local CLI Agents, providers, models, and Workspaces;
- technical owners defining Projects, requirements, decisions, assets, and delivery evidence;
- teams comparing real runs, benchmark evidence, spend, and quality;
- plugin and connector developers.

The product is not yet proven as a general nontechnical autonomous production system. Positioning must not claim complete autonomous delivery while roadmap items remain incomplete.

## Visual system

- Typography: Manrope for Latin UI, Alexandria for Arabic, Cascadia/Consolas stack for terminal/code.
- Palette: neutral product surfaces with indigo action accent and semantic emerald/amber/rose states.
- Shape: restrained 8–22 px radius scale; current token audit introduces a shared spacing/type/state scale.
- Icons: Lucide line icons, plus a simple Command glyph brand mark.
- Themes: light/dark and LTR/RTL are implemented.

## Strengths

- Local-first and evidence/provenance language differentiates the product from generic AI dashboards.
- Project, Run, Agent, Model, Workspace, Asset, Requirement, and Deliverable identities have concrete product meaning.
- Unknown, unavailable, estimated, and approval states are visible rather than hidden.
- Terminal and developer-tool visual language matches the verified execution foundation.

## Risks and clichés

- `AI`, `Fleet`, `OS`, `Command Center`, sparkles, robots, and generic model grids are crowded naming and visual territories.
- The fleet metaphor can overemphasize model inventory when the intended product is project/outcome production.
- `OS` may imply sandboxing, isolation, installer maturity, or broad platform support that is not complete.
- Dashboard savings/value language can overclaim unless provenance remains prominent.
- Current icon is functional but not distinctive enough for final identity.

## Naming constraints

A future owner decision should evaluate:

- differentiation from existing developer/AI/fleet/orchestration products;
- pronounceability and clarity in English and Arabic;
- repository, package, command, domain, and trademark collision screening;
- fit with project-first outcomes rather than model-first inventory;
- no implication of unsupported cloud service, security isolation, or autonomous completion.

No rename should occur before positioning approval and sourced collision research. `AI Fleet OS` remains the codename until then.

## Proof required for positioning

Claims should be backed by verified product evidence:

- local CLI/provider discovery and execution;
- canonical Runs, attempts, cancellation, output, artifacts, and provenance;
- Projects, Brain, requirements, decisions, assets, context, quality, and deliverables;
- measured/provider-reported/estimated/unknown cost and value separation;
- real benchmark/Arena/judge evidence;
- security and workspace boundaries.

Roadmap-only marketplace, installer, sandbox, cross-platform PTY, or complete autonomous production must not be marketed as done.
